The last page wasn’t enough

Building Teletext After Dark with Kiro

2miha · 25 November 2026

I wrote this for the Kiroween 2026 hackathon. Teletext After Dark is a browser mystery played on a fictional local TV station. Dial a page number, compare the live signal with the archive, and figure out what the station got wrong. I wanted the keypad to be part of the investigation, not just something that made the game look old.

The ending was too easy to broadcast

In the original Harbour Blackout case, you could send the correction once you had pieced the evidence together. That left a gap: the story asked players to be careful about what they believed, but the last interaction didn’t ask them to be careful about what they sent.

A rule Kiro could turn into a test

In Kiro IDE, I used a Requirements-First Feature Spec to turn that gap into an emergency broadcast puzzle. The player compares receiver fields with archive values, puts them in the right order, and chooses the right channel. One requirement made the difference: even if the values are right, a packet with the wrong field order must not go out. That’s easier to implement and test than “make the ending harder.”

The archive stays the archive

The spec also forced me to separate the live draft from the evidence. Switching English and Czech or reloading the page shouldn’t let a draft slip through the broadcast gate. The repo has deterministic smoke tests for these paths. My favourite part of using Kiro was being able to point to a rule in the spec and the test that proves it, instead of relying on a successful playthrough.

What was built with what

The original anthology and puzzle baseline were built with OpenAI assistance. Kiro IDE added the emergency-packet finale. Later touch and stability improvements were a separate QA pass. The Kiro requirements, design, and task checklist are checked into the public repo alongside the game and tests.
